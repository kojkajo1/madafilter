Common Mistakes When Changing Your Own Oil Filter (and How to Avoid Them)

Mada Filters & Automotive Oils — Syria

Changing your own oil filter is one of the most common DIY maintenance jobs. It looks simple in theory, but a few common mistakes can turn it into a real engine problem.

Mistake #1: Not cleaning the mating surface

Before installing the new filter, make sure the old rubber gasket didn't stay stuck in place. Missing this detail causes a serious oil leak.

Mistake #2: Over- or under-tightening

Over-tightening damages the rubber gasket; under-tightening causes leaks. The rule of thumb: hand-tighten until it seats, then a quarter turn more — no tools.

Other common mistakes

  • Not lightly oiling the rubber gasket with fresh oil before installing.
  • Using a filter that doesn't match your engine's specification.
  • Forgetting to check the oil level after startup.
  • Disposing of the old filter and oil improperly.
